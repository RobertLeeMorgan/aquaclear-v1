    - title: Restoring a Silted Pond
      content: |
        At a nature reserve in Pembrokeshire, our team was asked to restore a small pond that had become heavily choked with accumulated silt and reed growth.

        Following reed clearance, a silt pump was used to remove the accumulated material and increase the depth of the pond. The silt was pumped into large dewatering bags, allowing excess water to drain away while retaining the heavier material.

        The works were carried out progressively over several days, gradually removing many tonnes of silt and restoring valuable open water within the reserve.

    - title: Dewatering Many Tonnes of Silt
      content: |
        The dewatering bags were filled using the silt pump and then left to drain before further material was added.

        This process was repeated throughout the works, allowing the bags to gradually accumulate many tonnes of silt while excess water drained away. The approach provided a controlled and practical way to handle the material on site without immediately removing it from the reserve.

        As the pumping progressed, the depth of the pond increased and areas of open water were restored.

    - title: Reopening the Natural Brook
      content: |
        A grab bucket was also used to remove a 'silt island' that had accumulated in the middle of the watercourse.

        Removing this material opened up the natural brook feeding into the pond, improving the connection between the incoming watercourse and the restored open water.

        Combined with the silt pumping works, the clearance helped restore the pond's depth and improve the natural flow through the site.
