        This public pond had become heavily affected by excessive algae growth,

        Situated within a popular public space and surrounded by well-used

        increasingly noticeable to visitors and local residents.


        As the algae continued to accumulate, the once open waterbody became dominated by thick floating growth, significantly affecting its appearance. During warmer periods, decaying algae also produced an unpleasant odour around the surrounding paths and public areas. With the pond forming a prominent feature of the site, the local council began receiving regular complaints regarding both its appearance and smell.

      title: The Importance of Managing Algae
      content: Excessive algae growth can affect both the health of a waterbody and
        the wildlife it supports. In public ponds, the potential risks are
        greater because people and their pets are more likely to come into close
        contact with the water. Some blooms, particularly those involving
        cyanobacteria (blue-green algae), can produce toxins that may be harmful
        to people, dogs and other animals. Dense growth and subsequent
        decomposition can also reduce dissolved oxygen levels, placing fish and
        aquatic wildlife under additional stress. Heavy surface growth can also
        significantly detract from the appearance of a pond, particularly where
        the waterbody forms an important feature within a park or other
        well-used public space.

        In some situations, mechanical algae removal can provide a more
        practical and cost-effective solution than undertaking major works to
        address the underlying causes. Dredging a waterbody of this scale can
        carry a substantial price tag, while chemical treatment across such a
        large area can also be prohibitively expensive and may require repeated
        applications. Neither approach necessarily guarantees a long-term
        solution, particularly where the conditions driving algae growth
        originate beyond the waterbody itself.


        As this pond forms the final waterbody in the system before water reaches the sea, nutrients and other factors contributing to algae growth may originate much further upstream. Addressing the underlying cause could therefore require significant works across the wider catchment, with major investment in the pond itself offering no guarantee that the algae would not return.


        In these circumstances, regular mechanical clearance can provide a faster and considerably more economical approach to ongoing management. Rather than committing to costly large-scale interventions, algae can be removed as required to restore open water, improve the appearance of the site and remove accumulated organic material. The works can also be carefully timed around ecological constraints, including the bird nesting season, allowing the waterbody to be maintained effectively with relatively little disruption.
