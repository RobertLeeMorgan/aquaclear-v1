    eyebrow: Our Approach
    title: Invasive Weed Removal Methods
    description: |
      Invasive species require careful handling because cutting or disturbing the wrong plant in the wrong way can encourage further spread. We adapt our methods to the species, site, and extent of the infestation.
    items:
      - title: Cutting & Clearing
        content: |
          We cut invasive vegetation as deeply as practical and remove as much plant material as possible from the water. This helps restore open water while reducing the amount of material left behind to fragment and re-establish.

          Cut material is collected and, where appropriate, left temporarily on the bank to allow invertebrates to return to the water before the vegetation is removed from site.
        media:
          type: image
          src: ../../assets/images/hero/placeholder.jpeg
          alt: Mechanical cutting and removal of invasive aquatic weeds
      - title: Preventing Spread
        content: |
          Particular care is taken to minimise fragmentation during removal, as small pieces of some invasive plants can survive and establish new growth elsewhere.

          We remove as much cut material as possible from the watercourse and plan the handling and disposal of vegetation to reduce the risk of further spread and re-establishment.
        media:
          type: image
          src: ../../assets/images/hero/placeholder.jpeg
          alt: Mechanical cutting and removal of invasive aquatic weeds
      - title: Machinery Hygiene
        content: |
          Our machinery is cleaned and inspected after working with invasive species to remove plant fragments and reduce the risk of transferring invasive material between waterways.

          Careful cleaning between sites is an important part of working responsibly with invasive aquatic vegetation and helps prevent cross-contamination.

    groups:

          - title: Canadian Pond Weed - Nuttall's Waterweed - Curly Waterweed
            blocks:
              - type: text
                heading: Elodea Family
                content: |
                  These submerged weeds can form dense growth throughout the warmer months. Cutting and clearing can be used to reopen the watercourse, with the timing and frequency of works considered according to the site and growth pattern.

                  Cutting earlier in the season can help reduce growth during the warmer months, although a second cut may be required where vegetation becomes excessive again later in the season.
                media:
                  type: image
                  src: ../../assets/images/hero/placeholder.jpeg
                  alt: Canadian pond weed and other Elodea species
          - title: Floating Pennywort
            blocks:
              - type: text
                content: |
                  Floating pennywort can spread rapidly from banks and margins, forming dense floating mats across the surface of the water.

                  The plant can be cut away from the banks and collected using weed rakes before being moved onto nearby banks. After allowing time for invertebrates to return to the watercourse, the removed material can be taken away from the site to reduce the risk of recontamination.
                media:
                  type: image
                  src: ../../assets/images/hero/placeholder.jpeg
                  alt: Floating pennywort growing along a watercourse
          - title: Crassula - New Zealand Pygmyweed
            blocks:
              - type: text
                content: |
                  Crassula grows throughout the year in the damp margins of watercourses and can spread further when its short stems are cut and fragmented.

                  Where appropriate, grab buckets can be used to carefully peel layers of the plant from the affected area rather than relying on simple cutting, allowing the material to be removed for processing.
                media:
                  type: image
                  src: ../../assets/images/hero/placeholder.jpeg

          - title: Water Fern - Fairy Fern
            blocks:
              - type: text
                content: |
                  Water fern is less common in the UK but can become problematic once established. It can be peeled from affected areas using grab buckets or cut into larger mats that can then be collected and moved onto the bank for processing.
