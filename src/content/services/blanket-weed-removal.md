        content: |
          The simplest form of blanket weed control is mechanical removal from the surface of the water. Truxor machinery can skim and collect floating algae, depositing the removed material on nearby banks so that it can be cleared from the site.

          By removing algae from the surface while leaving submerged aquatic plant life largely undisturbed, this approach can help restore open water while retaining plants that contribute oxygen to the watercourse.

      - title: Addressing Recurring Algae Growth
        content: |
          Recurring algae problems can be associated with nutrient-rich conditions and accumulated silt within the watercourse. Where appropriate, removing silt from the bottom can help reduce the nutrients available to support future algal growth.

          Silt pumping and dredging can therefore form part of a longer-term approach where repeated blanket weed growth is linked to the condition of the watercourse, helping to reduce the likelihood of recurring algal blooms.

        title: Harmful Algal Blooms
        content: |
          Excessive algal growth can sometimes develop into harmful algal blooms (HABs), including blooms commonly associated with blue-green algae. These blooms can produce toxins that pose risks to people, livestock, fish, birds, and other wildlife that come into contact with affected water.

          Where a watercourse is affected by a suspected harmful algal bloom, appropriate testing, monitoring, and specialist environmental advice may be required alongside any physical removal or management work.
