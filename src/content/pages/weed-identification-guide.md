  title: Aquatic Weeds
  description: Submerged and surface plant-life that can be found in deep and shallow waters.
  items:
    - eyebrow: Nymphaea alba & Nuphar lutea
      title: White & Yellow Water Lily
      description: |
        <strong>Identification</strong>
        <strong class="text-accent">White Water Lily:</strong> Large white flowers (sometimes pink-tinged) on stalks just above the water. Rounded, leathery leaves up to 30 cm across float on the surface.

        <strong class="text-accent">Yellow Water Lily:</strong> Smaller, cup-shaped yellow flowers with more oval and smaller leaves.

        <strong>Description</strong>
        Widespread across UK waters, growing in depths up to 5m. Flowers from June to August. While lilies are often visually appealing and beneficial, excessive growth can blanket the surface, restricting light and other plants' growth. Periodic cutting or rhizome removal may be needed to maintain balance.
      image: ../../assets/images/weeds/waterlily.webp
      href: ''
    - eyebrow: Elodea Family
      title: Canadian Pond-weed & Nuttall's Water-weed
      description: |
        <strong>Identification</strong>
        <strong class="text-accent">Canadian Pond-weed:</strong> Freshwater herb with much-branched stems up to 3m long. Leaves are dark green and grow in whorls of three along the middle and terminal parts of the stem, becoming denser toward the ends.

        <strong class="text-accent">Nuttall's Water-weed:</strong> Similar appearance but with longer, more slender stems, often freely branched. Leaves are pale green, evenly spaced, typically in whorls of 3 or occasionally 4.

        <strong>Description</strong>
        This highly invasive perennial plant family originates from North America. In UK waters they grow rapidly in shallow areas, outcompeting native species, reducing biodiversity, and clogging waterways. Despite being labelled as "oxygenating plants", dense growth can cause oxygen level fluctuations harmful to fish and invertebrates.

        Both species are listed under Schedule 9 of the Wildlife and Countryside Act (England & Wales), making it an offence to plant them in the wild. Best management involves cutting in spring to hinder new growth and removing all cut material from the watercourse.
      image: ../../assets/images/weeds/canadianpondweed.webp
      href: ''
    - eyebrow: Potamogeton natans
      title: Broad-leaved Pond-weed
      description: |
        <strong>Identification</strong>
        <strong class="text-accent">Leaves:</strong> Floating, dark green, leathery, 5–10 cm long, pointed tips and rounded bases.

        <strong class="text-accent">Flower:</strong> Dull yellow-green on 5–10 cm stalks.

        <strong>Description</strong>
        A fast-growing perennial common in UK lakes, ponds, and canals. Grows in still freshwater, typically in water under 1m deep but can reach 2m. Flowers from May to September.
      image: ../../assets/images/weeds/broadleaf.webp
      href: ''
    - eyebrow: Lagarosiphon major
      title: Curly Water-weed
      description: |
        <strong>Identification</strong>
        Appears as a "floating rope" with branched stems and strongly curled leaves that alternate along the stem. Individual stems can reach up to 3m long.

        <strong>Description</strong>
        Native to South Africa, this invasive species thrives in UK waters. Similar in impact to the Elodea family, it rapidly outcompetes native species, causes biodiversity loss, and blocks water flow. Despite its oxygenating reputation, dense infestations can destabilize oxygen levels and harm aquatic life.

        There have been cases of Curly Waterweed completely filling large lakes in the UK and Ireland. It is listed under Schedule 9 of the Wildlife and Countryside Act, making its planting in the wild illegal.
      image: ../../assets/images/weeds/waterweed.webp
      href: ''
    - eyebrow: Lemnoideae
      title: Duck Weed
      description: |
        <strong>Identification</strong>
        Tiny floating plants, each 1.5–5 mm in diameter, forming dense yellow-green mats across water surfaces.

        <strong>Description</strong>
        Common in ponds, canals, and flooded gravel pits. Duckweed reproduces by division and can rapidly blanket entire surfaces, blocking light and oxygen exchange. Removal by hand is difficult due to its size.

        Aquaclear uses Truxor's specialized tools to scoop and remove duckweed effectively from watercourses.
      image: ../../assets/images/weeds/duckweed.webp
      href: ''
    - eyebrow: Stratiotes aloides
      title: Water Soldier
      description: |
        <strong>Identification</strong>
        <strong class="text-accent">Leaves:</strong> Rosettes of sword-shaped, serrated leaves growing from a central point and attached by long, thin strands from the waterbed.

        <strong class="text-accent">Flower:</strong> Single, three-petaled white (sometimes pinkish) flower held above water during summer.

        <strong>Description</strong>
        Native to Europe, these plants remain submerged most of the year, surfacing and flowering from June to August. Manageable in small numbers, but large populations can choke small waterways.
      image: ../../assets/images/weeds/watersoldier.webp
      href: ''
    - eyebrow: Alga
      title: Algae
      description: |
        <strong>Identification</strong>
        Appears as foam, scum, or mats on or below the water surface, usually bright green to yellowish in colour, often resembling a paint spill.

        <strong>Description</strong>
        Algal blooms occur from nutrient overabundance (from runoff), warm temperatures, and still water. These blooms can damage ecosystems by depleting oxygen and blocking light.

        <strong class="text-accent">Harmful Algal Blooms (HABs):</strong> Potentially toxic, often indistinguishable from non-toxic blooms, can harm or kill animals.

        <strong class="text-accent">Non-toxic Blooms:</strong> Still harmful, causing oxygen loss and disrupting ecosystems when decomposing. Control options include mechanical removal, improving water flow, and using barley straw rafts to limit growth.

        You can find out more about our work with blanket weed on our <a class="text-primary" href="/services/blanket-weed-removal">Blanket Weed Removal</a> page.

  title: In the Margins
  description: Emergent plant-life that can commonly be found in the margins and very shallow waters of a watercourse.
  items:
    - eyebrow: Sparganium Family
      title: Branched & Unbranched Bur-reeds
      description: |
        <strong>Identification</strong>
        <strong class="text-accent">Branched Bur-reed:</strong> Robust, erect plant up to 1.5m tall with long, spiked green leaves. Produces branched stems with separate male and female flower clusters and spiky fruit up to 1cm wide.

        <strong class="text-accent">Unbranched Bur-reed:</strong> Found in deeper waters (~1m), with narrower floating leaves and unbranched flowering spikes.

        <strong>Description</strong>
        Extremely common in UK lakes, ponds, and canals. Dense rhizomes form mats beneath the surface. Overgrowth can block flow and reduce depth, requiring regular cutting or rhizome removal.
      image: ../../assets/images/weeds/burreed.webp
      href: ''
    - eyebrow: Typha
      title: Bulrush / Cattail
      description: |
        <strong>Identification</strong>
        Tall plant (up to 3m) with long blade-like leaves and a straw-coloured stem topped with a dense brown cylindrical flower spike.

        <strong>Description</strong>
        Frequent in UK freshwater habitats, often growing alongside Common Reed. In rich soil, it can quickly become invasive and choke watercourses from the banks inward. Controlled by cutting or removing root matter.

        You can find out more about our work with bulrush on our <a class="text-primary" href="/services/bulrush-removal">Bulrush Removal</a> page.
      image: ../../assets/images/weeds/bulrush.webp
      href: ''
    - eyebrow: Phragmites
      title: Common Reed
      description: |
        <strong>Identification</strong>
        Tall plant with round, hollow stems typically 2m tall (occasionally up to 4m). Flat leaves taper to a point. Flowers form 20–60 cm clusters ("spikelets") each containing 1–6 flowers.

        <strong>Description</strong>
        Forms extensive reedbeds across UK wetlands, turning from yellow-brown in winter to green in summer. Flowers August–October. While vital habitats for birds, unmanaged reedbeds can overtake water areas and require regular maintenance to preserve balance and biodiversity.

        You can find out more about our work with reed beds on our <a class="text-primary" href="/services/reed-bed-control">Reed bed control</a> page.
      image: ../../assets/images/weeds/commonreed.webp
      href: ''
    - eyebrow: Hydrocotyle ranunculoides
      title: Floating Pennywort
      description: |
        <strong>Identification</strong>
        <strong class="text-accent">Leaves:</strong> Round to kidney-shaped with 3–7 lobes on long petioles (up to 35 cm).

        <strong class="text-accent">Flowers:</strong> Small pale greenish-white to pale yellow blooms in clusters of 5–13.

        <strong>Description</strong>
        A fast-spreading invasive species in UK waterways, growing from shallow margins into deeper water. Banned from sale in 2014 due to rapid spread. Complete removal from the watercourse is critical to prevent regrowth.
      image: ../../assets/images/weeds/floatingpennywort.webp
      href: ''
    - eyebrow: Hippuris vulgaris
      title: Mare's-tail
      description: |
        <strong>Identification</strong>
        <strong class="text-accent">Leaves:</strong> Grow in whorls of 6–12. Submerged leaves are thinner and longer than those above the surface.

        <strong class="text-accent">Stems:</strong> Solid, unbranched, often curved, up to 60 cm long, with 20–30 cm protruding above shallow water.

        <strong>Description</strong>
        A creeping perennial found in shallow water and mud flats. Most leaves are above water, but it roots underwater. Known methane emitter; dense growth can obstruct stream and river flow.
      image: ../../assets/images/weeds/marestail.webp
      href: ''
    - eyebrow: Equisetum sp.
      title: Horsetail / Snake Grass
      description: |
        <strong>Identification</strong>
        <strong class="text-accent">Leaves:</strong> Thin and spiky, arranged in whorls around the ridged central stem.

        <strong class="text-accent">Stems:</strong> Green, jointed, ridged (6–40 ridges), sometimes with whorled branches.

        <strong>Description</strong>
        Though superficially similar to Mare's-tail, it is unrelated. Common in shallow water and margins, it spreads rapidly and can become unsightly or obstructive if not managed.
      image: ../../assets/images/weeds/horsetail.webp
